Corporate Finance Formulas Summary

Free study page by Signal & Runway · September 2026

Corporate finance exams come down to a short list of formulas used correctly. This free page covers the core set: time value of money, the CAPM cost of equity, and WACC - with worked numbers so you can check your own. It is taken from our Corporate Finance Core Pack.

Time value of money: the five formulas everything builds on

Core formulas (r = rate per period, n = number of periods):
Future value: FV = PV × (1+r)n
Present value: PV = FV / (1+r)n
Perpetuity (level payment C forever): PV = C / r
Growing perpetuity (first payment C, growth g, r > g): PV = C / (r - g)
Annuity (level payment C for n periods): PV = C × [1 - (1+r)-n] / r

CAPM and the cost of equity

CAPM: re = rf + β × (rm - rf)
rf = risk-free rate; (rm - rf) = market risk premium; β = sensitivity of the stock's return to the market's.

Beta measures systematic (market-wide, undiversifiable) risk only. Firm-specific risk is diversifiable and earns no premium in CAPM. β = 1 moves with the market; β > 1 amplifies; β < 1 dampens.

Worked example

rf = 3%, market risk premium = 5%, β = 1.2: re = 3% + 1.2 × 5% = 9%.

Security market line: CAPM plotted as required return vs beta. A stock plotting above the line offers more return than its risk requires (underpriced); below the line, overpriced.

WACC - the firm's discount rate

WACC = (E/V) × re + (D/V) × rd × (1 - Tc)
E, D = market values of equity and debt; V = E + D; rd = pre-tax cost of debt; Tc = corporate tax rate. The (1 - Tc) term captures the tax deductibility of interest.

Worked example

Equity 60m (E/V = 60%), debt 40m (D/V = 40%), re = 9% (from CAPM), rd = 5%, tax 30%: WACC = 0.6 × 9% + 0.4 × 5% × 0.7 = 5.4% + 1.4% = 6.8%. Use 6.8% to discount projects of average firm risk.

RuleWhy it matters
Use market values, not book valuesBook equity can be far from what investors pay
WACC fits average-risk projectsRiskier projects need a higher hurdle; safer ones lower
After-tax cost of debtInterest shields taxes; ignoring it overstates WACC
Weights describe the target capital structureForward-looking, not today's snapshot

Capital structure intuition

Debt is cheaper than equity but raises financial risk, which pushes re up. Modigliani-Miller with corporate taxes: interest tax shields add value, so value rises with debt until distress costs offset the shield (trade-off theory). With no taxes and no frictions, capital structure does not change firm value.
