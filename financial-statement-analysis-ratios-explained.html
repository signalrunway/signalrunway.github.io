Financial Statement Analysis: Ratios Explained

Free study page by Signal & Runway · September 2026

Ratio analysis is not about memorizing formulas - it is about reading a company's statements in the right order and knowing what each number can and cannot tell you. This free page is the analysis framework section of our Financial Statement Analysis pack.

The ratio analysis framework

Learn ratios in families. Each family answers one question. Never quote a ratio without its family question and a comparison (prior year, peer, or sector norm).

3.1 Liquidity - can it pay near-term bills?

RatioFormulaRead
Current ratioCurrent assets / current liabilities>1 = short-term assets cover short-term debts; too high can mean idle assets
Quick (acid-test)(Current assets - inventory) / current liabilitiesStricter; inventory may not convert to cash fast
Cash ratio(Cash + marketable securities) / current liabilitiesMost conservative; stress scenario

3.2 Efficiency / activity - how hard do assets work?

RatioFormulaRead
Inventory turnoverCOGS / average inventoryHigher = leaner; too high risks stockouts
Days inventory (DIO)365 / inventory turnoverDays stock sits before sale
Receivables turnoverRevenue / average receivablesCollection speed
Days sales outstanding (DSO)365 / receivables turnoverRising DSO = customers paying slower - red flag
Payables days (DPO)365 / (COGS / average payables)How long the firm stretches suppliers
Cash conversion cycleDIO + DSO - DPODays cash is tied up in operations; shorter = better
Total asset turnoverRevenue / average total assetsAsset productivity; low-margin retailers need it high

3.3 Solvency / leverage - can it survive its debts?

RatioFormulaRead
Debt-to-equityTotal debt / total equityGearing; compare within industry
Debt ratioTotal liabilities / total assetsShare of assets funded by creditors
Interest cover (times interest earned)EBIT / interest expense<2 is uncomfortable; lenders watch this
Net debt / EBITDA(Debt - cash) / EBITDAYears of cash earnings to clear net debt; >3-4x is stretched for most sectors

3.4 Profitability - does it earn its keep?

RatioFormulaRead
Gross margin(Revenue - COGS) / revenuePricing power and production economics
Operating (EBIT) marginEBIT / revenueCore business profitability, pre-financing
Net marginNet income / revenueBottom line after everything
Return on equity (ROE)Net income / average equityWhat shareholders earn on their capital
Return on assets (ROA)Net income / average total assetsProfit per euro of asset base
Return on capital employed (ROCE)EBIT / (total assets - current liabilities)Pre-financing return on long-term capital; compare to WACC

3.5 Market ratios - what is the market paying?

RatioFormulaRead
P/EShare price / EPSPrice per euro of earnings; needs growth context (see PEG)
PEGP/E / expected EPS growth %~1 = growth roughly priced in
P/BShare price / book value per shareUseful for banks/asset-heavy; weak for intangibles
Dividend yieldDPS / share priceCash return; check payout cover
EV/EBITDA(Market cap + net debt) / EBITDACapital-structure-neutral; the workhorse multiple (section 7)

DuPont decomposition

DuPont turns one number (ROE) into a story about why. The 3-step version:

ROE = Net margin × Asset turnover × Equity multiplier
= (Net income / Revenue) × (Revenue / Avg assets) × (Avg assets / Avg equity)

So ROE can rise three ways: better margins (profitability), faster asset turns (efficiency), or more leverage (risk). The exam trick is always the same: a rising ROE driven by the equity multiplier is not an improvement in the business - it is an increase in risk.

The 5-step version splits net margin further:

ROE = (NI/EBT) × (EBT/EBIT) × (EBIT/Revenue) × (Revenue/Avg assets) × (Avg assets/Avg equity)
    = tax burden × interest burden × EBIT margin × asset turnover × leverage
Exam reflex: given a table of two years, decompose the change in ROE and name the driver. "ROE rose from 12% to 15%, but margin and turnover were flat while the equity multiplier rose from 1.8 to 2.4 - the improvement is leverage-driven and therefore riskier."
