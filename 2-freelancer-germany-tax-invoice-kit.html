Freelancer in Germany: invoices and VAT without the panic

Free guide by Signal & Runway · checked September 2026 · general information, not tax advice

Your first German freelance invoice decides whether the Finanzamt sees clean books or a problem. The rules are stable and learnable - but they are German, and the English summaries online are often outdated. This page covers the 2026 state of play.

The Kleinunternehmer rule (§ 19 UStG), 2026 thresholds

Since 1 January 2025 the small-business exemption applies when your net turnover did not exceed 25,000 EUR in the previous calendar year AND does not exceed 100,000 EUR in the current year. Both limbs must hold, and the years are calendar years.

  • If you qualify: your sales are genuinely VAT-exempt. You show no VAT on invoices, and instead must add a short exemption note (§ 34a UStDV). The trade-off: you cannot reclaim input VAT on your purchases.
  • If you cross 100,000 EUR mid-year: the exemption ends with the very sale that crosses the line - that sale itself becomes taxable. The old grace period (switch next year) no longer exists. Watch your running total near the boundary.
  • The § 14c trap: if you show VAT on an invoice while exempt, you owe that VAT to the Finanzamt anyway. Wrong invoice, real bill.

What every German invoice must contain

Full names and addresses of both parties, your Steuernummer or USt-IdNr, invoice date, a unique sequential invoice number, quantity and description of the service, date of delivery/performance, net amount, VAT rate and amount (or the exemption/reverse-charge note), and payment terms. Missing fields can cost your client their input-VAT deduction - which is how freelancers lose clients.
