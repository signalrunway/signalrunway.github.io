Munich Rent Check: is your rent above the legal cap?

Free guide by Signal & Runway · checked September 2026 · general information, not legal advice

Munich is one of Germany's designated tight housing markets, so the rent brake (Mietpreisbremse) caps most new letting rents at 10% above the local reference rent (ortsübliche Vergleichsmiete) from the official Mietspiegel. Munich's current Mietspiegel is the Mietspiegel für München 2025 - a fully rebuilt, qualified Mietspiegel approved by the city council in March 2025 (data snapshot January 2024). It covers roughly 500,000 privately financed flats. Many newcomers pay above the cap without knowing, because the official tools at mietspiegel-muenchen.de are German-only. Here is how the check works.

The four steps

  1. Find your Wohnlage. Munich grades every address into residential-area classes (einfach, mittel, gut, sehr gut). The city's official online map at mietspiegel-muenchen.de gives yours.
  2. Find your table row. The qualified Mietspiegel derives a reference rent per sqm from building age, size, fixtures and location, with a span rather than a single number. The city's online calculator does the lookup for you - in German.
  3. Apply the cap. For most new contracts in Munich the legal ceiling is the local reference rent plus 10%. Above that, the excess can usually be reclaimed.
  4. Check the exemptions. The same federal exemptions as everywhere in Germany decide most real cases: first letting after comprehensive modernization, modernization surcharge, the previous tenant's higher rent, new buildings (first use after 1 October 2014), and certain temporary furnished lets.

If your rent looks too high

Evidence first: contract, move-in date, sqm, building age, modernization documents, and a printout of your Mietspiegel result. For rent-increase letters on existing contracts, the qualified 2025 Mietspiegel is the binding reference landlords must cite, and you can refuse an increase above it. For advice, the Münchner Mieterverein offers membership with consultation and letter review for an annual fee; lawyers cost more but handle the whole case.
