Moving to Germany: the first 30 days, in the right order

Free guide by Signal & Runway · checked September 2026 · general information, not legal advice

German bureaucracy is sequential: almost everything needs the step before it. Doing things in the wrong order costs weeks. This is the order that works, and what each step realistically costs in 2026.

Week 1: the address unlocks everything

  1. Anmeldung (address registration). Book the Bürgeramt appointment the day you have a signed lease - in big cities slots can be weeks out. You must register within two weeks of moving in. You need the landlord confirmation (Wohnungsgeberbestätigung); without it, no registration. You leave with the Meldebescheinigung, the document everything else asks for.
  2. Tax ID (Steuer-ID). Issued automatically after Anmeldung and posted to your registered address - typically 2-4 weeks. Employers and banks ask for it; plan for the wait.

Week 2: money and coverage

  1. Bank account. Most banks want the Meldebescheinigung; some online banks onboard with just a passport and video ident. Having any IBAN early matters because rent deposits, salaries and insurance all move by SEPA.
  2. Health insurance. Mandatory from day one. Employed: pick a public insurer (TK, AOK, Barmer - around 14.6% plus a small insurer-specific surcharge, split with the employer). Freelancers and newcomers without a job: budget the full contribution yourself. Gaps in coverage get back-billed, so do not leave this floating.

Weeks 3-4: status and setup

  1. Residence permit (non-EU). Book the Ausländerbehörde appointment early - in Berlin and Munich the backlog is months, and the booking itself is free. Your entry visa keeps you legal until the appointment; the permit is what extends it.
  2. The rest. Liability insurance (a few EUR/month, the one Germans insist on), internet contract (2-4 week activation lead time), broadcast fee (Rundfunkbeitrag, 18.36 EUR/month per household - register proactively, they find you anyway), and, if you drive, anmeldung of the car.

The mistakes that delay everything

Starting the permit hunt before the Anmeldung; signing an internet contract before move-in (you pay for weeks of nothing); letting the landlord confirmation slip; and assuming any office will switch to English - bring translations or a German-speaking friend for the important appointments.
