Financial Accounting Ratios Cheat Sheet

Free study page by Signal & Runway · September 2026 · framework-agnostic (IFRS/US GAAP differences flagged in the full pack)

Every introductory financial accounting exam tests the same ratio families. This page is the complete ratio section of our Financial Accounting Core Pack, free: the formulas, what each one reads as, and the DuPont decomposition examiners love. Use the average balance (opening + closing)/2 for ROE, ROA and turnover ratios unless the question says otherwise.

The ratios exams actually test

Liquidity

RatioFormulaReads as
Current ratioCurrent assets / current liabilities> 1 means short-term assets cover short-term debts
Quick (acid-test) ratio(Current assets - inventory) / current liabilitiesSame, excluding the least liquid current asset

Profitability

RatioFormulaReads as
Gross margin(Revenue - COGS) / revenuePricing power and production cost control
Operating marginOperating income (EBIT) / revenueCore business profitability before financing and tax
Net marginNet income / revenueBottom-line profitability
ROENet income / average equityReturn on shareholders' money
ROANet income / average total assetsHow hard the asset base works

Leverage and coverage

RatioFormulaReads as
Debt-to-equityTotal liabilities / total equityFinancing mix; higher = more leveraged
Interest coverageEBIT / interest expenseHow comfortably profit covers interest; < 1.5 is fragile

Efficiency

RatioFormulaReads as
Inventory turnoverCOGS / average inventoryTimes inventory sells through per year
Receivables days (DSO)Average receivables / revenue x 365Days customers take to pay
Payables days (DPO)Average payables / COGS x 365Days the company takes to pay suppliers
Cash conversion cycleInventory days + DSO - DPODays cash is tied up in operations; lower is better
DuPont decomposition (classic exam question): ROE = net margin x asset turnover x equity multiplier = (net income / revenue) x (revenue / average assets) x (average assets / average equity). It splits ROE into profitability, efficiency, and leverage - use it to explain WHY ROE moved.
