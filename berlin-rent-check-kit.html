Berlin Rent Check: is your rent above the legal cap?

Free guide by Signal & Runway · September 2026 · general information, not legal advice

Berlin's rent brake (Mietpreisbremse) caps most new letting rents at 10% above the local reference rent from the official Mietspiegel. The current Mietspiegel was published by the Berlin Senate on 28 May 2026. Many expats pay above the cap without knowing it, because the official tools are German-only. Here is how the check works.

The four steps

  1. Find your Wohnlage. Berlin classifies every address into a residential area class (simple, medium, good). The Senate's official map at mietspiegel.berlin.de gives yours.
  2. Find your table row. The Mietspiegel table picks a reference rent per sqm from building age, fixtures and your Wohnlage, with a lower-middle-upper span.
  3. Apply the cap. For most new contracts in Berlin the legal ceiling is the local reference rent plus 10%. Above that, the excess can usually be reclaimed.
  4. Check the exemptions. Five exemptions decide most real cases: first letting after full modernization, modernization surcharges, the previous tenant's higher rent, new buildings (first use after 1 October 2014), and temporary furnished lets in specific cases.

If your rent looks too high

Evidence matters more than outrage: contract, move-in date, sqm, building age, modernization documents, and your Mietspiegel row printout. Berlin's free city service (Mietpreisprüfstelle) reviews cases at no cost; a Mieterverein membership (about 132 EUR/year) covers advice and letters; law firms and success-fee services take a cut.
